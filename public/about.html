About Louisiana911

Louisiana911 puts public incident feeds from around Louisiana on one map. It started as Caddo911, a map of Caddo Parish 911 calls.

This is not an emergency service. Information can be late, incomplete, changed, or removed. Don't use this site to decide whether to get help. In an emergency, call 911.

Lake Charles details

LCPD publishes closed calls, not live ones. A closed call isn't a finding that a crime happened, and the list covers LCPD only, not every agency in Calcasieu Parish.

Louisiana911 checks the public list every 30 minutes. Latest shows up to 150 calls from the past seven days, and every imported call is in History under its original date. LCPD doesn't publish units. Only the source's own map points are used; calls without one stay unmapped.

Map locations

Each source's location text is shown as published. Markers are placed only when a location can be matched with confidence. Approximate markers are larger and have a dashed outline.

Baton Rouge markers use the approximate point from the City-Parish traffic map. When that map is behind the incident list, Louisiana911 may place the marker from the published address range or cross street.

When New Orleans lists a block or intersection but gives a 0,0 point, Louisiana911 matches that same text to a map point and marks it approximate. The address shown is never more specific than what NOPD published.

What's hidden from New Orleans

The NOPD dataset includes officer activity and routine administrative calls. Louisiana911 leaves out officer-initiated calls, plus these routine call types:

  • Area Check
  • Business Check
  • Complaint Other
  • Disturbance (Other)
  • Fugitive Attachment
  • Incident Requested by Another Agency
  • Medical
  • Mental Patient
  • Return for Additional Info
  • Tow Impounded Vehicle (Private)

If one of these started as something more serious, such as theft, battery, burglary, robbery, or a protection-order violation, it stays visible under that original type.

Colors

Red is high severity, yellow is medium, blue is low, green is public service, and gray is custody. Red-and-white stripes mean medical. Louisiana911 assigns these colors from the call description; they are not official classifications. Reports uses the same colors.
